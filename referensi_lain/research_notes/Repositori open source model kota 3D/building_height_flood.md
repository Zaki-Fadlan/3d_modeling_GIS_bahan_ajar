# Open-source building height (no LiDAR) and better-than-threshold flood modelling for an Indonesian campus (UI Depok)

Research date: 2026-10-16. All claims below come from web fetches/searches in this session; items marked "(unverified, from background knowledge)" appear only in Inferences/Gaps. Several fetch results were summarised by a small model, so exact figures should be re-checked at the source before being put in teaching material.

## A1. Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal: accuracy, MAE, licence, caveats (the 880-building source)

### Takeaway
The published MAE of 1.5 m was measured only in North America, Europe and Japan; Global South (incl. Indonesia) accuracy is not quantified by Google, and an independent 3-city Global South study found the product tends to overestimate heights. Your own 1.87 m MAE against it is therefore a consistency check between two proxies, not a ground-truth accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Deep learning estimates per-pixel building presence, count and height from Sentinel-2; inference used stacks of 32 images centred on 30 June each year; heights are capped at 100 m; the raster is "only a relative measure (height above ground)"; use height only with the presence raster and mask low-confidence pixels. — [Google Research Open Buildings Temporal page](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/temporal/)
- Height MAE 1.5 m, "evaluated only in North America, Europe, and Japan"; "Ground truth data for heights was only available in the Global North", Global South evaluation mostly qualitative; large errors possible for tall buildings; no RMSE or correlation published. — [Google Research Open Buildings Temporal page](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/temporal/)
- Effective resolution about 4 m, rasters delivered at 50 cm; annual 2016-2023; licence CC BY-4.0 and ODbL v1.0 (user's choice). — [Google Research Open Buildings Temporal page](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/temporal/)
- Access: Earth Engine collection `GOOGLE_Research_open-buildings-temporal_v1`; direct GeoTIFF download from GCS bucket `open-buildings-temporal-data/v1` with a Colab notebook. — [Google Research page](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/temporal/); [Earth Engine catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_Research_open-buildings-temporal_v1)
- Method is a student-teacher HRNet: teacher uses high-resolution imagery to make labels, student learns from up to 32 Sentinel-2 frames; blog says height training data came mainly from US/Europe and Global South height evaluation relied on spot checks; roof position may be shifted, not orthorectified especially for tall buildings; buildings can appear to shift between years. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/open-buildings-25d-temporal-dataset-tracks-building-changes-across-the-global-south/)
- Independent evaluation: Watson and Elliott (Leeds), "Narrowing the gap for city building height predictions", Scientific Reports 2025, compared 1.5 m satellite-DEM heights (MAE under 1 m vs. reference), their own deep-learning model (MAE 2.2-7.0 m) and Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal in Nairobi, Kathmandu and Quito. The Google product improved on their DL model in two of three cities but "tended to overestimate building heights"; they recommend local evaluation against reference data. No Open Buildings MAE number was given on the page fetched. — [White Rose eprint 231111](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/231111/)
- Discrepancy: the blog describes 10 m per pixel input while the dataset page says 4 m effective resolution/50 cm raster; these are different things (input vs. model output grid), not necessarily a contradiction. — [blog](https://research.google/blog/open-buildings-25d-temporal-dataset-tracks-building-changes-across-the-global-south/) vs [dataset page](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/temporal/)
- A secondary review (VoxCity paper) lists the product with 4 m / 1.5 m (resolution / MAE) — it only repeats Google's figure. — [VoxCity arXiv 2504.13934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13934)

### Inferences
- For a defensible method section, report the 2.5D Temporal heights as "model-predicted, unvalidated for Indonesia, likely biased high" and cite Watson & Elliott 2025.
- A sensible local validation is a small field/photo sample (e.g. 30-50 UI buildings counted floors from Google Street View/Mapillary/on site) to compute your own bias and MAE; this converts the 1.87 m inter-product MAE into a real accuracy figure.
- Using median over several years (as you do) reduces temporal noise but not a systematic bias.

### Gaps
- No published Indonesia/Jakarta validation of the 2.5D Temporal heights was found.
- The Earth Engine catalog page was only surfaced in search snippets, not fetched in full.

## A2. Global/regional height products: GHS-BUILT-H, WSF3D, 3D-GloBFP, GlobalBuildingAtlas, Microsoft, Overture

### Takeaway
Only 3D-GloBFP (footprint-level, 2020, Zenodo) and GlobalBuildingAtlas (3 m, per-building, claimed global) give per-building heights usable for UI-scale work; GHS-BUILT-H (100 m) and WSF3D (90 m) are far too coarse for 1,000 buildings and are useful only as sanity checks. Microsoft footprints carry a height field that is -1 where missing; Overture has height/num_floors fields but the only height source documented in the material I found is US 3DEP lidar plus OSM tags.

### Cited Findings
- **GHS-BUILT-H R2023A**: 100 m grid, epoch 2018, average net building height (ANBH) in metres; inputs ALOS AW3D30, SRTM30 and a Sentinel-2 2017-2018 composite (shadow markers); reuse allowed with acknowledgement; ANBH = built-up volume / built-up surface. — [Earth Engine catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GHSL_P2023A_GHS_BUILT_H); [AWS Open Data registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ghs-built-h/); method paper doi:10.1080/17538947.2024.2390454 (Int. J. Digital Earth 2024) cited by the catalog.
- **WSF 3D** (DLR): 90 m global layers for average building height, building area, volume and fraction; from a modified World Settlement Footprint mask (Sentinel-1/2) plus TanDEM-X height-difference analysis along potential building edges; reference Esch et al., Remote Sensing of Environment 270 (2022) 112877. — [DLR GeoService WSF 3D](https://geoservice.dlr.de/web/datasets/wsf_3d)
- **3D-GloBFP** (Che et al., ESSD 16, 5357-5374, 2024): footprint-level heights for ~2020 from XGBoost with 114 features (Sentinel-1 VV/VH, Sentinel-2, SRTM, ALOS DSM and their nDSM difference, PALSAR, WorldPop, VIIRS, morphology); footprints mainly Microsoft; 10-fold CV R2 0.66-0.96 and RMSE 1.92-14.60 m across 33 subregions (Africa overall RMSE 9.87 m); vs 700 manually measured buildings in 14 cities R2 0.85, RMSE 11.01 m; largest errors for buildings 20 m and taller; no Southeast-Asia-specific accuracy reported; Indonesia present in national totals. Shapefiles on Zenodo: Asia [10.5281/zenodo.11397014](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11397014); paper CC BY 4.0 (the dataset-file licence was not stated on the page). — [ESSD article](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/5357/2024/essd-16-5357-2024.html)
- **GlobalBuildingAtlas (GBA.LoD1)**: per-building polygons (>2.75 billion), heights at 3x3 m, 2.68 billion buildings with predicted heights, height completeness >97%, height RMSE 1.5-8.9 m across continents, CC BY 4.0 shown on arXiv page; the abstract page did not give a download link or an Asia/Indonesia breakdown. — [arXiv 2506.04106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04106)
- **Microsoft GlobalMLBuildingFootprints**: yes, a height attribute exists — height estimated by a neural network from imagery, averaged per polygon, metres, "-1" when no estimate; not every building has it; licence CDLA Permissive 2.0; GeoJSONL (EPSG:4326) in quadkey files listed in `dataset-links.csv`; also on Planetary Computer; hosting moved July 2026 to `bfppub.blob.core.windows.net/$web/...`; a GeoJSON layer shows coverage of buildings with height. Indonesia height coverage could not be confirmed from the page. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints)
- An earlier Bing release reported 1.2B footprints with 174M height estimates (i.e. heights for a subset of regions). — [marksblogg summary / Bing blog search result](https://blogs.bing.com/maps/2023-06/Bing-Maps-Global-Building-Footprints-released)
- **Overture Maps buildings schema**: `height` (m, lowest to highest point, >0), `num_floors` (above ground, >0), `roof_height`, `min_height`, and `sources[]` per feature. — [Overture schema reference](https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/buildings/building/). Overture's first height release derived >6 million heights from USGS 3DEP lidar (US only) with Microsoft/OSM footprints. — [Overture guide / NSGIC summary](https://nsgic.org/knowledge-base/3dep-ftn-interest-group-meeting-overtures-building-heights-jan-28-2025). I found no evidence that Google Open Buildings supplies Overture height/num_floors.
- Other Sentinel-based CNNs: Sentinel-1+2 time-series CNN trained on 10 Dutch cities got RMSE 3.73 m, R2 0.61 (not Indonesia). — [arXiv 2307.01378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01378)

### Inferences
- A robust triangulation for UI: Google 2.5D Temporal (median) vs 3D-GloBFP (Zenodo Asia) vs Microsoft height (if non-negative) vs OSM levels, then report inter-product spread as an uncertainty band. Averages across products are not more accurate by construction (shared Sentinel/ALOS inputs), so spread is only indicative.
- 3D-GloBFP and Microsoft share Microsoft footprints, so a spatial join by centroid/IoU is feasible; GBA needs confirmation of the download location.
- GHS-BUILT-H/WSF3D can only validate the campus-mean height (one or two 100 m cells), not individual buildings.

### Gaps
- No quantitative GHS-BUILT-H or WSF3D accuracy was retrieved (paper not opened).
- GlobalBuildingAtlas download URL/format (likely a TUM/mediaTUM or Zenodo-style host) not verified.
- Microsoft height coverage in Indonesia and its accuracy not verified; Microsoft's height accuracy not found.
- Whether Overture's current releases include any non-US ML heights not found.

## A3. Height from imagery/DSM without LiDAR: nDSM, stereo, monocular deep learning, shadows; Indonesia/Jakarta literature

### Takeaway
Direct nDSM from open 30 m DSMs is too coarse for building-scale heights; the only Jakarta-specific work found used commercial 0.5 m stereo satellite DSMs. Monocular deep-learning repos exist (IM2HEIGHT lineage) but I did not verify installable, Indonesia-ready code; shadow-based height has no mature Python package (pybdshadow does the forward direction only).

### Cited Findings
- Jakarta: Lo, Lin, Daksiya, Shao, Chuang and Pan, "Efficient Building Inventory Extraction from Satellite Imagery for Megacities" (ASPRS) tuned a method on Taipei test areas then applied it to Jakarta (643 km2), extracting >1.247 million buildings from 0.5 m satellite stereo imagery; Taipei footprint-matching accuracy 48.8% one-to-one, 71.9% for buildings >8000 m2 floor area; Jakarta height validation was not visible in the snippet. — [NTU DR-NTU copy](https://dr.ntu.edu.sg/server/api/core/bitstreams/0e317f2f-bfd0-4f54-a6c3-244a17e4121e/content)
- IM2HEIGHT (Mou and Zhu 2018): fully residual convolutional-deconvolutional network predicting a height map from a single aerial image. — [arXiv 1802.10249](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10249). A code link is listed by SOTAVerified but the URL was not shown in my results.
- PhiSat-2 monocular building-height dataset and TSONet (Song et al., 2026), ~4.75 m imagery, open-access — [arXiv 2603.29245](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29245.pdf) (only seen in search summary).
- Number of stories from a monocular satellite image with a modified Mask R-CNN — [Remote Sensing 12(22):3833](https://0-doi-org.brum.beds.ac.uk/10.3390/rs12223833) (floors directly, relevant to your floor-count formula).
- COSMO-SkyMed single-image SAR height regression, MAE ~2.20 m in Munich (commercial SAR, not free) — [arXiv 2507.08096](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08096); the authors note challenges in high-rise Asian metropolises.
- Shadow approach: pybdshadow (Python) takes building heights and computes shadow geometry via suncalc-py; it does NOT estimate height from shadows. — [pybdshadow docs](https://pybdshadow.readthedocs.io/). A thesis PHYSHADE (physics-informed NN from aerial imagery + shadow segmentation) reports RMSE ~1.9 m / MAE ~1.5 m on test tiles — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_3e638270-231d-40ee-9dad-86d76b7ee195?preview=1) (not Indonesia, code availability not checked).
- nDSM concept used operationally inside 3D-GloBFP (ALOS DSM minus SRTM at 30 m as a feature, not as the height itself). — [ESSD article](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/5357/2024/essd-16-5357-2024.html)
- FABDEM (bare-earth Copernicus GLO-30 with buildings/trees removed by ML): built-up-area MAE 1.61 m to 1.12 m vs GLO-30 — [EGU22-8994](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-8994.html); note that GLO-30 minus FABDEM is a coarse (30 m) building/canopy height proxy (my inference, below). Licence not confirmed in this session.

### Inferences
- A cheap, defensible own-derivation for UI: (1) GLO-30 DSM minus FABDEM (30 m) is only a screening indicator, not per-building height; (2) 8 m DEMNAS is DSM-like, but there is no matching DTM at the same resolution, so nDSM from DEMNAS needs a DTM (FABDEM, resampled) and will be noisy (unverified quantitatively).
- Practical best path remains product triangulation plus a street-level floor-count sample; floors x 3.5-4 m is more defensible than metric deep-learning heights for low-rise campus buildings.
- Forward shadow modelling (pybdshadow) is useful in teaching as a visual check: render shadows from estimated heights and compare with the shadows visible in a satellite image of known acquisition time.

### Gaps
- No open GitHub repository for monocular building-height estimation was verified (clone/licence/weights/pretrained for tropical cities); IM2HEIGHT code URL not confirmed.
- No Indonesia-specific open dataset of building heights or floors beyond the commercial-imagery Jakarta study; no verified use of Copernicus/AW3D nDSM for Jakarta buildings.
- DEMNAS licence, vertical accuracy and DSM/DTM nature not confirmed (search returned only access guides; BIG Downloader QGIS plugin 4.0.2, 2026-06-01, exists — [QGIS plugin page](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/big_downloader_toolbox/)).

## B1. Open-source hydrologic/hydraulic tools with Python access (HAND, ANUGA, SFINCS, LISFLOOD-FP, Itzi, CADDIES, HEC-RAS)

### Takeaway
For a 3 km2, 8 m DEM teaching notebook the realistic ladder is: (1) HAND (pysheds/richdem/whitebox) for a drainage-referenced, physically ordered threshold; (2) SFINCS via HydroMT-SFINCS (fast, rainfall-capable, but Windows exe/Docker needed and GPL/freeware terms) or ANUGA (pure pip/conda Python package with C extensions) for real rain-on-grid dynamics. LISFLOOD-FP, Itzi and CADDIES are valid but have heavier setup; HEC-RAS is free but not open and Windows-only.

### Cited Findings
- **pysheds**: "Simple and fast watershed delineation in python"; flow direction, accumulation, catchments, river network; D8 and D-infinity; includes `compute_hand`; GPL-3.0; pip/conda-forge; Python 3 only; ~517 commits, 49 open issues; benchmark on 36M cells: fill_depressions 27.1 s. — [GitHub mdbartos/pysheds](https://github.com/mdbartos/pysheds). NASA/ASF's global 30 m HAND dataset (GLO-30 HAND) was built with pysheds, usable as a reference — [NASA Earthdata tutorial](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/tutorials/global-30-m-hand); [ASF STAC](https://stac.asf.alaska.edu/collections/glo-30-hand).
- HAND tutorials: a Python/Jupyter HAND flood-risk workflow (RichDEM + WhiteboxTools, 30 m DEM, stream threshold, inundation classes) — [Analytics Vidhya 2024](https://analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2024/11/flood-risk-assessment); pysheds watershed + HAND flood zones — [Meteomatics blog](https://www.meteomatics.com/en/blog/flood-modelling-part/); method explainer by Tarboton — [CUAHSI exercise](https://hydrology.usu.edu/dtarb/CUAHSIVirtualUniversity/2019/ProgrammingHANDExercise.pdf).
- **SFINCS** (Deltares): "super-fast open-source flood model"; storm surge, fluvial, pluvial and wave-driven flooding; "designed to run in minutes instead of days"; source GPL v3.0; pre-built Windows executable as freeware from download.deltares.nl/sfincs; Linux via compile or Docker image `deltares/sfincs-cpu`; recent subgrid-correction paper (2025). — [SFINCS docs](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/); [GitHub Deltares/SFINCS](https://github.com/Deltares/SFINCS). The GitHub README mentions both GPL-3.0 and a "Deltares Freeware" licence, so exact licensing needs checking in the LICENSE file.
- **HydroMT-SFINCS**: plugin to build/update/analyse SFINCS models from CLI or Python; GPL-3.0; install from conda-forge in a clean env; does not include the SFINCS executable; v2 introduced a component-based API (`sf.grid.create()`, `sf.elevation.create()`) with a migration guide, so older tutorials use different names; can set infiltration and Manning roughness from land-use maps. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Deltares/hydromt_sfincs); [docs](https://deltares.github.io/hydromt_sfincs/latest/); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/hydromt-sfincs/); [SFINCS model setup page](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/example.html)
- **ANUGA** (Geoscience Australia/ANU): open-source shallow water equation solver for floods and tsunami, Python 3 with C/Cython extensions, `conda install anuga` from conda-forge; examples, validation tests and Colab notebooks (anuga-clinic repo); licence is in LICENSE.txt (not named on the page fetched). — [GitHub GeoscienceAustralia/anuga_core](https://github.com/GeoscienceAustralia/anuga_core)
- **LISFLOOD-FP 8.x**: GPL v3.0; 8.2 adds GPU + multiwavelet dynamic grid adaptivity (2.0-4.5x speed-ups reported), test cases in the paper are tsunami flooding; no Python wrapper or rainfall module found. — [GMD 18, 9827 (2025)](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/9827/2025/gmd-18-9827-2025-assets.html); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/record/4073010)
- **Itzi**: 2D raster flood model with GRASS GIS as back-end, coupled to SWMM drainage; damped partial inertia equation for surface flow and Green-Ampt infiltration; needs a projected location; accepts raster time series of rainfall; Courty et al. 2017, GMD 10, 1835. — [PyPI itzi](https://pypi.org/project/itzi); [GMD paper](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/10/1835/2017/)
- **CADDIES / WCA2D / caflood** (Univ. Exeter): cellular-automata pluvial flood framework, MIT licence, CPU/GPU via OpenCL, dual drainage; build instructions via CMake/Visual Studio, so not Python-native. — [Exeter CADDIES](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/research/centres/cws/resources/caddies/)
- **HEC-RAS 2D** rain-on-grid: free but not open source; Python automation via `ras-commander` (pip, CLB Engineering) and `rivia` (Windows-only, cannot create 2D mesh); QGIS plugin QCT HEC-RAS Manager; rain-on-grid applies rainfall uniformly to the 2D area and losses must be handled before applying precipitation. — [ras-commander docs](https://ras-commander.readthedocs.io); [rivia docs](https://rivia.readthedocs.io/en/stable/guide/introduction.html); [Hydrolearn tutorial](https://serc.carleton.edu/serc/external_collections/hydrolearn/2429.html)
- Jakarta/Ciliwung precedent: a BTOPMC distributed model on the Ciliwung (checking at Katulampa, Depok, Manggarai; NSE 56.81% at Katulampa) and a HEC-RAS study of the January 2014 Ciliwung flood with levee/normalisation scenarios; a Rainfall-Runoff-Inundation (RRI) model driven by station rainfall and SRTM for the January 2020 event. — [Garuda record](https://garuda.kemdikbud.go.id/documents/detail/1274099); [Jakarta Flood Advisory (RMS)](https://pier.rmsi.com/download/Jakarta%20Flood%20Advisory.pdf) and [sciforum paper](https://sciforum.net/paper/view/14317) (as listed in search results; details not opened).
- DEM caveat: FABDEM (bare earth GLO-30) improved flood extents vs other global DEMs in a Vietnam study; reference EGU22/Fathom papers. — [Fathom FABDEM Vietnam](https://www.fathom.global/academic-papers/using-fabdem-in-vietnam/)

### Inferences
- With an 8 m DSM-like DEM, buildings act as walls/obstacles and vegetation as raised ground; for rain-on-grid you either burn building footprints in as high-roughness/blocked cells (a legitimate use of your building layer) or switch to a bare-earth-like terrain; both choices should be stated as assumptions. This is why a pure threshold on a filled DSM barely reacts to OSM-water connectivity: filling removes the very depressions that matter for pluvial ponding.
- Feasibility ranking for a ~3 km2, 8 m grid (about 47,000 cells): all engines are trivially fast at this size; the real constraint is installation and licence friction. ANUGA (conda, Python) and HydroMT-SFINCS + Docker/Windows exe are the best fits; SFINCS is likely most familiar to hydrology courses because HydroMT automates forcing and masks (unverified claim of pluvial example notebook names in docs; not located).
- HAND is not a dynamic model: it needs a drainage network (pysheds/accumulation threshold plus OSM river/lakes burned in) and a rating assumption (stage above drainage), but it is a clear teaching step beyond a global threshold and takes seconds in pysheds.
- Suggested notebook ladder: threshold (existing) -> HAND stage scenarios (0.5/1/2 m above lake/Ciliwung) -> rain-on-grid with ANUGA or SFINCS (design storm, building mask) -> compare extents with InaRISK/Aqueduct.

### Gaps
- Exact current versions of pysheds, ANUGA, HydroMT-SFINCS were not retrieved (release sections appeared empty in fetched summaries).
- No verified HydroMT-SFINCS pluvial example notebook URL; no verified ANUGA rainfall-on-DEM example; no verified TELEMAC, floodpy, richdem or WhiteboxTools HAND page content (not searched in depth).
- Whether pysheds is actively maintained for current NumPy/Python versions is not established.
- Licence for ANUGA not found in text.

## B2. Rainfall forcing and external flood hazard references for Depok/Jakarta (BMKG, CHIRPS, IMERG, InaRISK, Aqueduct, Fathom)

### Takeaway
Satellite rainfall (IMERG, CHIRPS) is usable but systematically underestimates Indonesian extremes; for a teaching design storm, BMKG station records (e.g. the 377 mm/day Halim record of 1 Jan 2020) or an IDF-based design storm are more defensible. InaRISK offers official hazard layers via ArcGIS ImageServers (with an unofficial QGIS plugin); Aqueduct is 10 km, too coarse for campus use.

### Cited Findings
- January 2020 event: BMKG Halim Perdanakusuma recorded 377 mm/day on 31 Dec 2019-1 Jan 2020 (stated as highest since records began in 1866 by one source); TMII 335 mm and Jatiasih (Bekasi) 259 mm; flooding hit 41 areas in Jakarta and satellite cities; landslide deaths in Jakarta, Depok and Bogor. — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2020/01/01/not-ordinary-rain-worst-rainfall-in-over-decade-causes-massive-floods-in-jakarta); [Jakarta Post 2 Jan 2020](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2020/01/02/capital-welcomes-new-decade-drenched)
- IMERG over the Indonesian Maritime Continent (2016-2020 gauges): captures climatology (KGE above 0.4) but underestimated 9 of 13 extreme indices and overestimated 4; high-intensity indices (R95p, R50mm, RX1day) underestimated. — [Ramadhan et al. 2022, Remote Sensing](https://doaj.org/article/013d94e1981b4e5ca35ceb43efadf59d); [UGM-hosted Indonesia evaluation](https://acadstaff.ugm.ac.id/karya_files/evaluation-of-gpm-imerg-products-for-extreme-precipitation-over-indonesia-dcfcd07e645d245babe887e5e2daa016). Da Silva et al. 2021 argue ground-sampling error may have led to underestimation of IMERG skill for Maritime Continent extremes. — [Reading CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/98688/9/2021EA001738.pdf)
- InaRISK: BNPB serves layers as ArcGIS ImageServers on inarisk.bnpb.go.id (prefix "INDEKS" for analysis, e.g. `INDEKS_BAHAYA_BANJIR`; prefix "layer" for overlay/integration); download of data/maps/risk documents from the portal; the unofficial QGIS plugin "InaRISK to QGIS" fetches and clips these layers. Endpoint liveness and terms were not verified. — [ITS guest-lecture deck (BNPB, 2024)](https://www.its.ac.id/tgeofisika/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2024/03/Materi-Kuliah-Tamu-Teknik-Geofisika-ITS_Pengenalan-inaRISK_23Maret2024.pdf); [QGIS plugin](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/inarisk_to_qgis/)
- WRI Aqueduct Floods: riverine and coastal inundation depth, current plus 2030/2050/2080, ~10 km resolution (Earth Engine `WRI/Aqueduct_Flood_Hazard_Maps/V2`, 10,000 m), return periods 10 to 500 years in the riverine layers; released 2020, simulated without flood protection; licence not stated in catalogs seen. — [Earth Engine catalog](https://developers.google.cn/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/WRI_Aqueduct_Flood_Hazard_Maps_V2); [WRI datasets](https://datasets.wri.org//datasets/aqueduct-floods-hazard-maps)
- Fathom: FABDEM and flood hazard are Fathom-linked academic products; the commercial "FABDEM+" product exists; no free Fathom hazard map for Depok was found. — [Fathom](https://www.fathom.global/?p=17108)

### Inferences
- A defensible teaching design storm: take a daily total from a BMKG Depok/Katulampa/Halim record (or IDF if available from Pusair/PUPR), disaggregate with an alternating-block or SCS Type hyetograph, and state clearly it is an assumed storm (my inference; no Depok IDF source was retrieved).
- IMERG half-hourly can give event shape (timing) for a real event (e.g. 1 Jan 2020) but peak intensity will be biased low; scale to station totals.
- Treat InaRISK as a qualitative cross-check (it is an index, not depth) and Aqueduct only as context (10 km).

### Gaps
- No verified Python recipe for IMERG download (earthaccess product short names), CHIRPS, or BMKG data access/licence (BMKG online data may need registration; not checked).
- No Depok-specific IDF curves or UI-campus flood extent study found (Indonesian-language search suggested: "banjir Kampus UI Depok situ", "genangan UI Depok").
- No InaRISK flood hazard depth numbers for the UI campus extracted; Fathom/FABDEM licence not confirmed.
- TELEMAC, floodpy and other "flood-mapping" GitHub repositories (e.g. Sentinel-1 flood mapping) were not researched; Sentinel-1 flood extent mapping is a possible validation source for the 2020 event but no source was retrieved.
