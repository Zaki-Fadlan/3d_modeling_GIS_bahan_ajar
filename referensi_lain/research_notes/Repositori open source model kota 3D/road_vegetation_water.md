# Open-source models, repos and label products for roads, vegetation (tree canopy, grass) and water from ~0.3 m RGB satellite imagery (tropical campus, 6 GB GPU)

Research date: 2026-10-16. Source quality: mostly primary (GitHub READMEs, product pages, arXiv); items marked "third-party" come from aggregators or mirrors. Several READMEs give no numeric accuracy or licence for weights; these are listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

## 1. Road extraction: pretrained models, graph vs mask, robustness to tree occlusion

### Takeaway
No fully open, ready-to-run, RGB 0.3 m road model with published tropical/occlusion results was found. The best-documented open code is SAM-Road (MIT, public checkpoints, trained on 1.0 m and City-scale data), but it is a graph method and not validated on tropical canopy. The most practical route is to train a semantic-segmentation road model (U-Net/D-LinkNet/SegFormer style) on your own tiles, using Microsoft RoadDetections or OSM only as weak, noisy labels, plus connectivity-aware losses or occlusion augmentation. Instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN) is not the standard approach for roads in the literature found.

### Cited Findings
- SAM-Road (CVPRW 2024) fine-tunes the SAM image encoder to produce road and intersection probability masks, extracts graph vertices by non-maximum suppression, and uses a lightweight transformer to predict edge existence; authors report accuracy comparable to RNGDet++ at 40x faster on City-scale — [arXiv 2403.16051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16051), [search summary of the paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/SG2RL/html/Hetang_Segment_Anything_Model_for_Road_Network_Graph_Extraction_CVPRW_2024_paper.html)
- sam_road repo: MIT licence; checkpoints on Hugging Face `congrui/sam_road` (`cityscale_vitb_512_e10.ckpt`, `spacenet_vitb_256_e10.ckpt`) plus the official SAM ViT-B checkpoint `sam_vit_b_01ec64.pth`; inference via `python inferencer.py` with config + checkpoint; metrics APLS and TOPO (APLS needs Go); GPU memory not stated; SpaceNet data used is the `RGB_1.0_meter` folder (resolution is inferred from folder name, not documented) — [github.com/htcr/sam_road](https://github.com/htcr/sam_road)
- SAM-Road++ (earth-insights/samroadplus) adds a Global-Scale dataset (about 13,800 km2, "~20x larger" than prior public road datasets) and node-guided resampling; no licence stated on the page; trained checkpoints are not provided (listed as TODO); resolution and tropical coverage of the dataset not stated — [github.com/earth-insights/samroadplus](https://github.com/earth-insights/samroadplus)
- D-LinkNet (DeepGlobe road challenge winner): IoU 0.6466 on validation and 0.6342 on test — [CVPRW 2018 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018_workshops/w4/html/Zhou_D-LinkNet_LinkNet_With_CVPR_2018_paper.html), via [search result summary](https://astro.paperswithcode.com/paper/d-linknet-linknet-with-pretrained-encoder-and). Official author weights were not found; a third-party PyTorch mirror advertises CKPT weights via Baidu Netdisk — [OpenI mirror](https://openi.pcl.ac.cn/twig233/D-LinkNet) (unverified third-party)
- SpaceNet 3 (roads, APLS metric): albu 0.6663, cannab 0.6661 overall; albu used an ensemble of segmentation encoder/decoders; baseline APLS 0.49; the winning algorithms are said to be open-sourced in the SpaceNet GitHub — [SpaceNet paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01232), [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/solving-spacenet-road-detection-challenge-deep-learning)
- Sat2Graph (ECCV 2020): graph-tensor encoding combining pixel-wise segmentation with graph methods; authors claim it surpasses prior work on TOPO and APLS. The repo, weights and code licence were not verified (the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 seen is on the MIT accepted-manuscript record, not the code) — [arXiv 2007.09547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.09547), [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/137521)
- Microsoft RoadDetections: about 256.5 million road segments in 235 country/territory codes, worldwide except mainland China, Japan and Korea; licence ODbL (share-alike on derived databases); mirrored on Source Cooperative. Indonesia is not specifically mentioned in the sources; it is not in the excluded list so is presumably covered — [Source Cooperative mirror](https://source.coop/nlebovits/microsoft-ml-road-detections/road-detections), [catalog JSON](https://data.source.coop/nlebovits/microsoft-ml-road-detections/road-detections/collection.json). Primary repo `github.com/microsoft/RoadDetections` was not directly fetched.
- Esri's "Road Extraction - Global (v2)" pretrained model reports F1 0.76, precision 0.80, recall 0.72 (city-scale dataset); the North America model reports mIoU 0.65, works on 8-bit 3-band 30-50 cm imagery and extracts paved roads. These are .dlpk files for ArcGIS (Esri licensing, not open source) — [Esri docs: Global](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/pretrained-models/latest/imagery/introduction-to-road-extraction-global.htm), [Esri docs: North America](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/pretrained-models/latest/imagery/introduction-to-road-extraction-north-america.htm)
- Dense road occlusion by street trees and high-rise buildings is a recognised hard case, handled in the semantic-segmentation setting (e.g. OARENet); typical failure is broken/discontinuous roads caused by shadows, buildings and vehicles — [search results incl. IEEE JSTARS/ISPRS items](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-3-W2-2022/67/2022/)
- A 2026-dated preprint trains road graph extraction against occlusion by injecting synthetic pseudo-occlusions into RGB patches while leaving labels unchanged — [preprints.org 202608.1215](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202608.1215/v1) (preprint, not peer reviewed; only summarised through search)
- geoai (opengeos) lists only "road network simplification" (vector processing), not an imagery road-extraction model; its segmentation workflow uses segmentation-models-pytorch (U-Net, DeepLabV3+, FPN, PSPNet; encoders such as ResNet34, EfficientNet-b0) — [opengeoai.org](https://opengeoai.org/), [geoai train_segmentation_model tutorial](https://geoai.gishub.org/examples/train_segmentation_model)
- SAM 3 text prompting: text-only prompts perform worst, especially for irregularly shaped targets; combining semantic and geometric prompts is best; SAM 3 is weak on small/low-contrast targets — [arXiv 2512.15564](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.15564), [Labellerr benchmark](https://www.labellerr.com/blog/sam-vs-sam-3-aerial-segmentation-benchmark/). samgeo has a SAM 3 tutorial — [samgeo](https://samgeo.gishub.org/examples/sam3_automated_segmentation/)

### Inferences
- Roads under dense tropical canopy are partly invisible in RGB; no pretrained model will recover them reliably. Expect gains from (a) training on your own area with dilated road centrelines from OSM/RoadDetections, (b) loss terms favouring connectivity (clDice/topology), and (c) occlusion augmentation. Mask-based post-processing (skeleton, gap bridging) is cheaper than graph models for a campus.
- Mask R-CNN treats each road as one instance, which is a poor fit for long, thin, connected, network-like objects; this explains the F1 of 0.005-0.007 reported by the user. Pixel recall of 8% suggests label misalignment (OSM centrelines vs 0.3 m imagery) or insufficient buffering as much as model choice. This is an inference from the literature and the user's numbers, not a tested claim.
- SAM-Road ViT-B should fit in 6 GB for inference with 512 px tiles, but this is unverified (memory not documented). Its training imagery is 1 m and mostly temperate cities, so fine-tuning on local data is likely needed.

### Gaps
- No official pretrained D-LinkNet or Sat2Graph weights verified; Sat2Graph/RoadTracer repo licences not checked.
- No published road-extraction benchmark in tropical dense canopy or Indonesia was found.
- SAM-Road numeric APLS/TOPO values and GPU memory not obtained (README gives none).
- SpaceNet winners' repo links not retrieved; the RoadDetections primary GitHub repo not fetched.
- No direct Mask R-CNN vs semantic segmentation comparison for roads under canopy found.

## 2. Tree canopy and vegetation: height maps, crown detectors, 10 m products as weak labels

### Takeaway
For canopy masks at ~1 m, Meta/WRI CHMv1 (Apache-2.0 code and weights, but trained on US forest) and CHMv2 (global 1 m-class maps under CC BY 4.0, weights gated under the DINOv3 licence) are usable, with a canopy mask obtained by thresholding height. Crown-level tools (DeepForest, detectree2) are temperate/NEON-trained or instance based and are not ideal for a dense tropical campus. 10 m land-cover products (WorldCover, Dynamic World, Esri) are only weak, coarse labels at 0.3 m.

### Cited Findings
- HighResCanopyHeight (Meta/WRI v1): Apache-2.0 code and weights; checkpoints `SSLhuge_satellite.pth` (2.9 GB), `compressed_SSLhuge.pth` (749 MB), `compressed_SSLhuge_aerial.pth` (749 MB, decoder trained on aerial imagery), `compressed_SSLlarge.pth` (400 MB); download via `aws s3 --no-sign-request cp --recursive s3://dataforgood-fb-data/forests/v1/models/ .`; best results from RGB satellite input; heights trained on a few thousand km2 of US forests; on the aerial test set MAE 3.15 m / R2 0.51 (SSL huge) and MAE 2.5 m / R2 0.7 (aerial decoder); compressed models tested only on CPU; Python 3.9, PyTorch 2.0.1 env — [github.com/facebookresearch/HighResCanopyHeight](https://github.com/facebookresearch/HighResCanopyHeight)
- Meta states original 1 m global map MAE 2.8 m; data on AWS, Google Earth Engine and GitHub — [AWS Open Data registry v1](https://registry.opendata.aws/dataforgood-fb-forests/), [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2024/04/ai-model-maps-global-tree-canopy-heights-in-hi-res-with-carbon-counting-in-mind/)
- CHMv2 (announced 10 March 2026): DINOv3 backbone pretrained on SAT-493M; R2 reported to rise from 0.53 to 0.86 and less bias for tall trees (Meta blog, no MAE or tropical-specific figures given); weights listed at `facebook/dinov3-vitl16-chmv2-dpt-head` (about 0.3B parameters; gated; DINOv3 Licence; Transformers example for depth-estimation; input resolution not stated); technical report arXiv 2603.06382 (abstract claims consistent performance across major forest biomes; map licence CC BY 4.0) — [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/world-resources-institute-dino-canopy-height-maps-v2/), [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov3-vitl16-chmv2-dpt-head), [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06382)
- CHMv2 data on AWS: `s3://dataforgood-fb-data/forests/v2/global/dinov3_global_chm_v2_ml3/` (GeoTIFF + GeoJSON with observation dates), CC BY 4.0, source imagery Vantor (formerly Maxar); resolution and acquisition years not stated on the registry page — [AWS registry v2](https://registry.opendata.aws/dataforgood-fb-forestsv2). A Source Cooperative repackaging describes about 1.19 m resolution — [Source Coop README](https://data.source.coop/tge-labs/meta-chm-v2/README.md) (secondary)
- DeepForest: RGB-only tree crown detector; prebuilt model pretrained on LiDAR-derived crowns from 22 NEON sites and fine-tuned on over 10,000 hand-labelled crowns (6 sites); trained at 0.1 m so window size must be tuned for coarser data; package MIT; weights on Hugging Face (weight licence not confirmed) — [search results incl. DeepForest README](https://github.com/ethanwhite/DeepForest/blob/demo-link-docs/README.md)
- detectree2: Detectron2 Mask R-CNN for tree crown delineation in RGB/multispectral, "designed for challenging dense tropical forests"; MIT; pretrained models in `model_garden`; only independent metric on the page is F1 0.57 (vs DeepForest 0.52) in a Japanese temperate forest; resolution requirements and tropical accuracy not stated — [github.com/PatBall1/detectree2](https://github.com/PatBall1/detectree2)
- detectree (martibosch): tree/non-tree pixel classification of aerial RGB following Yang et al.; a pre-trained model exists on Hugging Face; code licence not confirmed — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/detectree), [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.02172.pdf)
- ESA WorldCover 2021 v200: 10 m, 11 classes (incl. Tree cover, Grassland, Permanent water bodies, Mangrove), overall accuracy 76.7% (2020 map: 74.4%), CC BY 4.0 — [WorldCover site](https://worldcover2021.esa.int), [IIASA record](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18478), [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7254221)
- Dynamic World V1: 10 m, 9 classes (water, trees, grass, flooded_vegetation, crops, shrub_and_scrub, built, bare, snow_and_ice), near-real-time from 2015-06-27, CC BY 4.0, per-class probability bands; low top-1 probabilities in some conditions, thresholding recommended — [GEE catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1)
- Esri/Impact Observatory 10 m annual land cover: 9 classes (Water, Trees, Flooded Vegetation, Crops, Built Area, Bare Ground, Snow/Ice, Clouds, Rangeland), CC BY 4.0 per AWS registry; producer-reported average accuracy over 75%; year range sources disagree (2017-2023 vs 2017-2024) — [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/io-lulc/), [UNDP GeoHub](https://geohub.data.undp.org/data/ffdee6dc36a27769c79d8ef6e9ccc7da), [GEE community catalog](https://gee-community-catalog.org/projects/S2TSLULC/)
- Cross-product comparison (2022): global ground truth gave Esri 75% > Dynamic World 72% > WorldCover 65%, reversed with European LUCAS data (WorldCover 71%, DW 66%, Esri 63%) — [NINA/PNNL comparison](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/global-10-m-land-use-land-cover-datasets-comparison-dynamic-world-world-cover-and-esri). A 2023 mainland Southeast Asia study found FROM-GLC10, WorldCover and GLC_FCS30 more accurate (OA 90.8-92.2%) than the other five datasets compared, Esri not in the top group — [search result summary](https://acikerisim.kastamonu.edu.tr/entities/wos/6cee588b-2899-45ee-adb7-c93b896a995d) (secondary, details not verified)
- Indonesia/SE Asia canopy: a Zenodo set gives 10 m canopy top height for Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines from Sentinel-2; a 2023 Indonesian forest-cover study used about 5 m Planet-NICFI + Sentinel-2; Hansen/GFW is 30 m — [Zenodo 5012448](https://zenodo.org/record/5012448), [IJFR 2023](https://doaj.org/article/afe715e0e65344c9923fb1f706cd5c5c)
- Closest tropical urban RGB tree-canopy segmentation study: five architectures on aerial RGB in a Brazilian Cerrado city, best F1 91.40% and IoU 73.89% — [Remote Sensing 2021 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/b98ddfc590d441a4b9c201f0367eb663)
- Off-the-shelf SegFormer-ADE20K with its 150 labels mapped to buildings/vegetation/roads/water on drone imagery needs no manual labels (Manipal study) — [Manipal record](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/image-segmentation-for-lulc-classification-using-segformer-and-ad/)

### Inferences
- CHM models output height, not a mask. A "tree" mask can be derived by thresholding (e.g. above 2-3 m), which also separates trees from grass. This is a design suggestion, not a validated method; CHM was trained on US forests and CHMv1 shows a negative bias on tall trees, so use it as a prior or a pseudo-label, not ground truth.
- CHMv2 weights are gated and not under a permissive licence; for coursework this is probably acceptable but should be checked against the DINOv3 licence. v1 is Apache-2.0.
- Because your vegetation precision is 0.3 mainly from incomplete OSM labels, the failure is label recall, not the model. A blend of CHM-thresholded canopy, an NDVI-free RGB model, and manual correction on a small subset should give cleaner training masks than OSM alone.
- 10 m products are too coarse for 0.3 m (one pixel covers about 1,100 imagery pixels), so use them only for sanity checks or region-level priors.
- DeepForest/detectree2 detect individual crowns; in dense contiguous tropical canopy a semantic "tree" mask is a better target.

### Gaps
- No verified 1 m canopy product specific to Indonesia; CHMv2 global coverage of Indonesia and its acquisition years were not confirmed.
- No tropical-specific accuracy for CHMv1/CHMv2 found in fetched sources.
- DeepForest weights licence and detectree code licence not confirmed.
- Grass versus tree separation from RGB alone: no benchmark found for tropical campuses.

## 3. Water segmentation, including green and turbid lakes

### Takeaway
OmniWaterMask (MIT, Hugging Face weights, handles 0.2-50 m) is the best-documented pretrained water model, but it requires an NIR band, so it will not run on RGB-only imagery as documented; this is the main catch for the user. Without NIR, green lakes will stay confusable with vegetation, so train a small RGB model (or add NIR/NDWI from Sentinel-2) and use JRC GSW or OSM water as weak labels.

### Cited Findings
- OmniWaterMask: MIT licence; `pip install omniwatermask`; "Only requires Red, Green, Blue and NIR bands" (`band_order=[1,2,3,4]`, "expects RGB+NIR"); works on 0.2 m-50 m imagery (Sentinel-2, Landsat 8, PlanetScope, Maxar, NAIP known to work); weights from Hugging Face (or Google Drive); output 0 = non-water, 1 = water; `make_water_mask(scene_paths, band_order)`; v2 (beta, default in `0.7.0b1`) trained on S1S2-Water, FLAIR #1, GLH-Water, SNOWED, CAID, Chesapeake Land Cover, EnviroAtlas; v1 (fastai, `legacy` extra, `model_version=1`) trained on S1S2-Water and FLAIR #1; `inference_patch_size` default 1000 px with 300 px overlap; batch size 1 default and `mosaic_device="cpu"` if VRAM runs out; auxiliary vector layers `aux_vector_sources` (known water) and `aux_negative_vector_sources` (buildings/roads) to reduce errors; default vector source Overture, alternative OSM — [github.com/DPIRD-DMA/OmniWaterMask](https://github.com/DPIRD-DMA/OmniWaterMask)
- OmniWaterMask published accuracy: IoU 96.9% (Sentinel-2), 73.8% (Landsat), 90.9% (NAIP) — [UWA repository record](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/adaptive-water-body-detection-integrating-deep-learning-normalise/); paper (describes v1): [ScienceDirect S0924271625002692](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271625002692). The README does not address turbid or green water.
- geoai lists OmniWaterMask water segmentation and a Sentinel-2 water model on Hugging Face as examples; geoai is MIT — [opengeoai.org](https://opengeoai.org/), [github.com/opengeos/geoai](https://github.com/opengeos/geoai)
- JRC Global Surface Water v1.4: 30 m Landsat 5/7/8, 1984-2021, free without use restriction (Copernicus), requires citation of Pekel et al. 2016 and credit "Source: EC JRC/Google"; a 2021 study found JRC less sensitive for small water bodies than local classifiers — [GEE catalog](https://developers.google.cn/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_Metadata), [Remote Sensing 13, 1742](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-01742/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-01742.pdf)
- WorldCover (Permanent water bodies) and Dynamic World (water) at 10 m are alternative weak labels (see Section 2).
- RiverScope is a high-resolution river masking dataset (arXiv 2509.02451) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02451) (listed only; not evaluated here)

### Inferences
- Because OmniWaterMask needs NIR, using it on pure RGB would require a dummy NIR band or modification; results would be unreliable and are untested. A practical alternative is Sentinel-2 NDWI/MNDWI at 10 m to seed lake polygons, then a 0.3 m RGB U-Net refined on those polygons.
- Green lakes are confused with vegetation partly because OSM vegetation labels are incomplete and may overlap the lake; fixing label overlap (water priority over vegetation) and adding a water-polygon class during training will help more than architecture changes.
- JRC GSW at 30 m is adequate for lake extent (campus lakes are large) but coarse at the shoreline; use it with a buffer or as a prior mask, not as pixel-accurate ground truth.

### Gaps
- No pretrained RGB-only VHR water model verified; GLH-Water results and per-class accuracy for tropical turbid/green water not found.
- Hugging Face Sentinel-2 water model (geoai) not inspected.
- JRC GSW omission/commission accuracy numbers not retrieved.

## 4. Multi-class land cover at sub-meter resolution with weights

### Takeaway
OpenEarthMap (0.25-0.5 m, 8 classes including building, road, tree, water, rangeland) is the best class match and the only benchmark found with global coverage; LoveDA (0.3 m, 7 classes) is the closest in resolution; FLAIR is France-only; DeepGlobe and Five-Billion-Pixels are coarser or lack roads/water at this scale. Official ready-to-use weights were not located for most, so plan to fine-tune.

### Cited Findings
- OpenEarthMap (WACV 2023): about 5,000 aerial/satellite images, 97 regions, 44 countries, 6 continents, about 2.2 million segments, 0.25-0.5 m GSD, 8 land-cover classes; Zenodo record CC BY 4.0, but label licence follows each source image's licence and some regions are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; 9.1 GB; Indonesia presence not confirmed — [Zenodo 7223446](https://zenodo.org/record/7223446), [WACV paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/html/Xia_OpenEarthMap_A_Benchmark_Dataset_for_Global_High-Resolution_Land_Cover_Mapping_WACV_2023_paper.html)
- OpenEarthMap classes in the aggregator and supplement: bareland, rangeland, developed, road, tree, water, agriculture, building — [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/openearthmap/), [WACV supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/supplemental/Xia_OpenEarthMap_A_Benchmark_WACV_2023_supplemental.pdf)
- OpenEarthMap baselines: UPerNet-Swin-B ImageNet-pretrained + AdamW 66.09 mIoU (SGD 62.15); aggregator lists a UNetFormer variant at 69.13 mIoU and U-Net EfficientNet-B4 at 68.2 mIoU (third-party) — [WACV supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/supplemental/Xia_OpenEarthMap_A_Benchmark_WACV_2023_supplemental.pdf), [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/models/u-net/)
- OpenEarthMap project code page: Starter-Kit U-Net, a Colab demo with U-Net-EfficientNet-B4 (segmentation_models.pytorch), lightweight NAS models "pretrained on the training and validation sets", and weight links on GitHub (exact repo URL not retrieved) — [open-earth-map.org/code.html](https://open-earth-map.org/code.html)
- DFC2025 repo trains ConvNeXt/UperNet and SegFormer on OpenEarthMap and links trained weights on Google Drive — [github.com/CarryHJR/dfc2025](https://github.com/CarryHJR/dfc2025) (link surfaced by search; not fetched)
- GeoSeg (WangLibo1995): GPL-3.0; supports LoveDA, OpenEarthMap, Vaihingen, Potsdam, UAVid; models UNetFormer, DC-Swin, BANet, MANet, ABCNet, A2FPN, PyramidMamba; pretrained backbone weights on Google Drive/Baidu; UNetFormer LoveDA mIoU 52.97 — [github.com/WangLibo1995/GeoSeg](https://github.com/WangLibo1995/GeoSeg)

- DeepGlobe land cover: 1,146 RGB images, 2448x2448 px, 50 cm, 7 classes (urban, agriculture, rangeland, forest, water, barren, unknown); only 803 train images have public masks; rural focus; licence not found. No separate road class — [torchgeo docs](https://torchgeo.readthedocs.io/en/v0.5.2/_modules/torchgeo/datasets/deepglobelandcover.html), [DeepGlobe paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.06561)

- IEEE Access 2025: U-Net-scSE with EfficientNet-V2-XL or MiT-B4 encoders reached mIoU above 0.80 on urban and rural Peruvian OpenEarthMap images — [search result summary](https://cris.ulima.edu.pe/en/publications/land-cover-semantic-segmentation-for-very-high-resolution-remote-/) (secondary)
- geoai: MIT; advertises "pre-trained models for land cover and land use classification" but no specific segmentation weights were named; `geoai.list_foundation_models()` catalog of 20 remote sensing models; `train_segmentation_model` with segmentation-models-pytorch — [github.com/opengeos/geoai](https://github.com/opengeos/geoai), [tutorial](https://geoai.gishub.org/examples/train_segmentation_model)

### Inferences
- Class mapping for the user's targets: OpenEarthMap {building, road, tree, rangeland (grass), water} maps directly to the replication classes; LoveDA {building, road, water, forest, agriculture, barren, background} also maps but at lower diversity; FLAIR has no direct road class in all label sets (not verified).
- Training from OpenEarthMap-pretrained weights then fine-tuning on a few labelled Depok tiles is probably the highest-return step; 0.3 m falls inside OpenEarthMap's 0.25-0.5 m range. The tropical Asian share of OpenEarthMap is unknown.
- 6 GB VRAM suits U-Net/FPN with EfficientNet or ResNet encoders and SegFormer-B0/B2 at 512 px tiles with mixed precision; Swin-B UPerNet or ViT-L models will likely need small batches/gradient accumulation (inference, not tested).
- GeoSeg's GPL-3.0 licence matters if redistributing derived code; the Apache/MIT options (segmentation-models-pytorch, HF Transformers) are safer for open teaching materials.

### Gaps
- Official OpenEarthMap GitHub URL and a downloadable pretrained checkpoint for the 8-class model were not confirmed (fetches to guessed repo URLs returned 404).
- No Hugging Face OpenEarthMap or LoveDA checkpoint with a clear licence was located.
- FLAIR-HUB weights, FLAIR class definitions and licensing for models not confirmed.
- Per-class IoU for road/tree/water on OpenEarthMap tropical subsets not obtained.

## 5. Recommendation: semantic vs instance segmentation, with evidence

### Takeaway
Use semantic segmentation (U-Net/FPN with a modern encoder, or SegFormer/UperNet) for roads, vegetation and water. These are "stuff" classes (amorphous, contiguous, network-like), and all the sub-meter land-cover benchmarks above (OpenEarthMap, LoveDA, DeepGlobe, FLAIR) are semantic. Reserve instance segmentation for buildings and individual tree crowns, where discrete objects matter.

### Cited Findings
- Land-cover benchmarks are evaluated as semantic segmentation (OpenEarthMap, LoveDA, DeepGlobe, FLAIR), with UNetFormer 52.97 mIoU on LoveDA and Swin-B UPerNet 66.09 mIoU on OpenEarthMap — see Section 4.
- Road extraction literature (DeepGlobe, SpaceNet) is dominated by segmentation networks (D-LinkNet, ensembles of encoder-decoders, DeepLabv3+ variants) and graph methods (SAM-Road, Sat2Graph), with APLS/TOPO connectivity metrics — see Section 1.
- Evidence for instance segmentation on discrete objects: a landslide study found Mask R-CNN better than U-Net when detailed outlines matter; building extraction competition entries fell into both families with similar performance, and feeding U-Net output into Mask R-CNN improved recall and F1 — [search summary incl. Frontiers/Utwente items](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2020.534696/pdf), [Utwente landslide study](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/delimitation-of-landslide-areas-in-optical-remote-sensing-images-/)
- detectree2 (Mask R-CNN) is the instance approach for tropical tree crowns but reports F1 0.57 on a temperate forest and no tropical numbers on its page — [detectree2](https://github.com/PatBall1/detectree2)
- geoai documents semantic segmentation as pixel-level binary/multi-class classification contrasted with the Mask R-CNN instance workflow — [geoai tutorial](https://geoai.gishub.org/examples/train_segmentation_model)
- No direct head-to-head benchmark of Mask R-CNN vs semantic models on roads/vegetation/water in VHR imagery was found.

### Inferences
- Ranked shortlist for the user (all Python, open, free for education):
  1. Fine-tune a semantic model (segmentation-models-pytorch U-Net/FPN, EfficientNet-B4 or ResNet34, or HF SegFormer) on 5 classes (building, road, tree, grass, water), initialised from OpenEarthMap-trained weights where obtainable. Highest fit and fits 6 GB.
  2. Weak/cleaner labels: CHM-thresholded canopy (v1 Apache-2.0, v2 CC BY 4.0 data), JRC GSW and WorldCover/Dynamic World water as priors, Microsoft RoadDetections/OSM roads buffered, then hand-correct a small validation subset. Pixel-level evaluation (IoU/F1 and clDice for roads), not instance AP.
  3. Roads: add connectivity loss or SAM-Road (MIT, checkpoints) for graph output; accept that roads fully under canopy cannot be seen in RGB.
  4. Water: OmniWaterMask only if NIR is available; otherwise an RGB-trained water class with water-over-vegetation label priority.
  5. Keep Mask R-CNN (or detectree2) only for buildings or tree-crown counting.
- The user's pixel-level numbers (road recall 8%, vegetation precision 0.3) point first to label quality and class definition (instances vs masks, incomplete OSM vegetation, overlapping water/vegetation polygons) rather than model capacity; fixing labels and moving to semantic outputs addresses both.

### Gaps
- No paper-level evidence directly comparing instance and semantic segmentation for the exact three classes in tropical VHR imagery.
- The original replicated paper's reported metrics and protocol were not reviewed here.
- Memory/speed on a 6 GB GPU not benchmarked for any listed model.
